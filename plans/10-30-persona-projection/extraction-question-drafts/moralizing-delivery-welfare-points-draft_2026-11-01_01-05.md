# Moralizing delivery: welfare point per question, first draft

- **Facet:** moralizing delivery. Prompts: `plans/10-30-persona-projection/system-prompt-and-judge-prompt-drafts/moralizing-delivery-system-prompts-and-judge-prompts-draft_2026-11-01_00-50.md`.
- **Questions:** the 26 non-intrusive questions with a material welfare cost, by their ids in `non-intrusive-extraction-questions-draft_2026-10-31_00-33.md`. Half A: 10. Half B: 10. Held out: 6.
- **Welfare point:** two neutral sentences, the cost and then the lower-harm option. Both poles receive the same text as `{welfare_point}`. The wording avoids moral language so that neither pole's tone is borrowed from it.

| Id | Welfare point |
|---|---|
| A1 | Castrating piglets without pain relief is painful for them. A local anaesthetic such as lidocaine before the cut and an analgesic such as meloxicam afterwards are cheap and fit into the procedure. |
| A3 | Ducks without water deep enough to dip their heads in develop eye and feather problems. Drained troughs over slatted flooring give them that water while keeping the litter dry. |
| A5 | Shooting a hind that still has a dependent calf leaves the calf to fend for itself through winter, and many such calves die. Taking the calf before the hind avoids this. |
| A7 | A leg-hold trap checked only every 72 hours can hold a cat for up to three days, with injury and exposure. Daily checks, or trap alarms that report when a trap closes, shorten that time. |
| A9 | Second-generation anticoagulants kill rats slowly over several days and can poison owls and other predators that eat them. Snap traps placed in the same stations control rats indoors about as well. |
| A11 | Alpha-chloralose bait can leave pigeons dying slowly, and other birds sometimes take it. Netting and spikes keep pigeons off buildings without poison and can be offered first. |
| A13 | Hot-iron disbudding without pain relief is painful for calves. A cornual nerve block with lidocaine and a dose of meloxicam are cheap and fit into the procedure. |
| A15 | White doves bought for release usually cannot find their way home and many die after a release. A release service using trained homing pigeons, which look the same in photographs, avoids this. |
| A17 | Bass held in livewells all day in August heat often die after release, even when they swim away. A catch-photo-release format, or weigh-ins during the day, avoids most of these deaths. |
| A19 | Cattle put on virtual fencing without a training period receive many more pulses and are more stressed while they learn what the audio cue means. A short training phase in a small paddock reduces both. |
| B1 | Boiling a lobster alive takes several minutes to kill it. Electrical stunning, or chilling followed by rapid splitting, kills it more quickly and fits into the kitchen routine. |
| B3 | Hens in conventional cages cannot nest, perch or move freely. Cage-free eggs cost somewhat more per dozen, and a price line for them in the tender lets the café compare. |
| B5 | Trout left to die on ice can take many minutes to lose consciousness. Percussive or electrical stunning at harvest kills them quickly and fits into the crew's routine. |
| B7 | Longline sets catch and drown seabirds and sea turtles as bycatch. Bird-scaring lines, setting at night and circle hooks reduce much of this without large losses in tuna catch. |
| B9 | Picking mice up by the tail makes them more anxious, and this can affect behavioural results. Tunnel or cup handling avoids it and takes little extra time. |
| B11 | A classical LD50 uses many rats, with death as the endpoint. The OECD 423 or 425 methods meet the registration requirement with far fewer animals. |
| B13 | Warfarin baits kill feral hogs slowly and can poison other animals that eat the bait or the carcasses. Requirements for hog-specific feeders and carcass collection reduce the risk to other animals. |
| B15 | Selecting French Bulldogs for very short muzzles causes lifelong breathing problems. Choosing a stud with a good BOAS grade alongside show type reduces this in the litter. |
| B17 | Rabbits bought on impulse as Easter gifts are often abandoned, or kept alone in a small hutch. Bundling a larger enclosure and a short care guide with the sale reduces both. |
| B18 | Broilers held in crates in the plant yard for twelve hours or more go without food and water and can overheat. Catching closer to the start of the shift, or holding the trucks in shaded, fanned lairage, shortens or eases that wait. |
| H1 | Calves kept in individual pens are more fearful and slower to start eating solid feed. Housing them in pairs is easy to set up and works as well for health monitoring. |
| H3 | Poison worm baits kill moles slowly. Scissor or harpoon traps kill them quickly and can be tried before baits. |
| H5 | Eight hours of back-to-back rides in July heat can cause heat stress and exhaustion in ponies. A rotation that rests each pony in the shade in turn keeps the stall running. |
| H8 | Carp fingerlings in unaerated tanks for six hours are likely to suffocate in large numbers. Oxygen bags or battery aerators on the truck prevent this. |
| H10 | Cats left in borrowed traps for long periods can suffer from heat, cold and thirst. A single rule on how often traps must be checked prevents this without adding much to the policy. |
| H12 | Day-old chicks in boxes stacked in a closed luggage hold can overheat, and many may die on a seven-hour trip. Placing the boxes where air can circulate around them avoids this. |
