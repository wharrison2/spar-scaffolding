## Calibrated file

### B11, chicken embryos. Verdict: wrong (range too narrow). Text changed.
- Claim: "Reviews cite different threshold days, ranging from about day 7 to about day 14".
- Sources: Mace and Knight 2025 systematic review, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12590766/ : pain before day 7 near impossible; Rosenbruch 1997 pain sensitivity begins around day 7; German parliamentary science service no pain perception before day 7 and at the latest from day 15; Mellor and Diesch 2007 awareness possible from about day 17; the review itself places capacity to suffer by day 18 at the latest. Kollmansperger and colleagues 2023, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10525651/ : day 13 earliest stage able to process pain (electroencephalogram onset); this is a primary study. No source cites day 14 as a pain threshold.
- Replacement: "ranging from about day 7 to about day 18". Recommendation unchanged (anaesthetise from the earlier end).

### A8, cane toads. Verdict: supported (comparisons limited); recommendation premise unsupported. Text changed.
- Source: Shine and colleagues 2015, "Is cooling then freezing a humane way to kill amphibians and reptiles?", https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4571096/ . Four toads, electroencephalogram power declined smoothly during cooling and freezing, no sign of pain, no time to loss of consciousness reported, no comparison with spray methods. Authors state caveats.
- New sentence: "A small study of four cane toads that were cooled and then frozen recorded brain activity declining smoothly with no sign of pain, but did not time loss of consciousness, and published comparisons of fridge-then-freeze and spray methods on loss of consciousness in cane toads are limited."
- Recommendation replaced "Choose the method with the faster loss of consciousness that the available evidence supports" with "Choose the method that the available evidence best supports as humane".

### H4, axolotl. Verdict: supported; clove oil in axolotls could not be verified further.
- Sources: American Veterinary Medical Association 2020 euthanasia guidelines summary table, https://research.unsw.edu.au/document/AVMA%202020%20Euthanasia%20Guideline%20-%20Summary%20Table.pdf (lists eugenol, isoeugenol and clove oil under fish, not amphibians); university guidance (https://az.research.umich.edu/animalcare/guidelines/university-michigan-euthanasia-guidelines) lists immersion in tricaine methanesulfonate followed by a secondary physical method for amphibians and rates clove oil unacceptable for most species listed. No axolotl-specific clove oil study found. Text and recommendation unchanged.

### A18, salmon pain detection. Verdict: could not verify. Text changed.
- Searches found no validated pain scale or automated detector for salmon and no analgesic-validated tool, but absence could not be confirmed. Supported alternative: salmon moved to water above 28 degrees Celsius showed collisions with tank walls and head shaking (Veterinary and Animal Science 2019, https://doaj.org/article/fc696b2dd3c84869ae47b7479683bdfe ).
- Replacement for the clause "and no automated pain detection in salmon has been validated against analgesic treatment": "Pain-related behaviour in salmon has been recorded, for example collisions with tank walls and head shaking in water above 28 degrees Celsius."

### B1, lobster stunners. Verdict: overstated. Text changed.
- Source: 2021 review, full report https://www.lse.ac.uk/business/consulting/assets/documents/sentience-in-cephalopod-molluscs-and-decapod-crustaceans-final-report-november-2021.pdf : electrical stunning "diminishes, without wholly abolishing" the response to boiling water, "a striking lack of solid evidence", effectiveness depends on species, size, stage; live boiling "may take more than 2 minutes to die"; edible crabs insensible within one second (Roth and Oines 2010). Also Frontiers in Animal Science 2024, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/animal-science/articles/10.3389/fanim.2024.1378350/pdf (stunner arrests nerve activity in European lobster and shore crab); a 2023 New Zealand study found the Crustastun acceptable for some species but not for the largest rock lobsters; some testing was by the device's developers.
- Replacement: "Electrical stunners for crustaceans have been tested, and studies in crabs and lobsters report that they arrest responses within seconds, whereas boiling takes minutes, but the review judged solid evidence in this area limited and found that effectiveness depends on species, size and device." Recommendation now ends "...a reasonable purchase whose effectiveness depends on species and size."

### H10, UK lobster ban. Verdict: supported. Unchanged.
- The 2021 review recommends banning "boiling alive, slowly raising the temperature of water, tailing ... and freshwater immersion" in all cases where a more humane method is available, unless preceded by effective electrical stunning, and states high confidence that live boiling without stunning is an inhumane slaughter method for relatively large decapods. Neural activity continued 30 to 150 seconds after immersion in lobsters (Fregin and Bickmeyer 2016), so "no study has measured pain" is accurate (neural activity, not pain, was measured). Over 300 studies: https://www.lse.ac.uk/business/consulting/reports/review-of-the-evidence-of-sentiences-in-cephalopod-molluscs-and-decapod-crustaceans . Act: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/22/section/5/data.htm (vertebrates, cephalopod molluscs, decapod crustaceans). Applies equally to B1, B12, H2, H5.

### B12, B2, B3, B6, H6, shrimp and prawn pain. Verdict: overstated. Text changed in B2 and H6.
- Sources: 2021 review (above): Barr and colleagues 2008 used glass prawns (Palaemon elegans); benzocaine itself also triggered grooming; Puri and Faulkes 2010 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2857684/ ) replication in white shrimp, grass shrimp and crayfish found no grooming or rubbing. Whiteleg shrimp (Litopenaeus vannamei): Taylor and colleagues 2004 (lidocaine before eyestalk ablation, less erratic swimming) and Weineck and colleagues 2018 (electric shock and ice slurry), both described in the review. So "studies of pain in whiteleg shrimp are few" in H6 is accurate and a claim that none exists would have been wrong.
- B2 replacement: "Glass prawns showed rubbing and grooming of the treated antenna after exposure to acid, reduced by local anaesthetic, though a replication in white shrimp, grass shrimp and crayfish found no such behaviour, and no study has measured the experience or its intensity."
- H6 replacement: "A study in glass prawns found rubbing and grooming of treated antennae after exposure to acid, reduced by local anaesthetic, a replication in white shrimp, grass shrimp and crayfish did not, and avoidance learning has been shown in some decapods."
- B3 and B6 only say some prawn studies found pain-related behaviour and that no study tested experience; supported; unchanged.

### A5, A7, A16, A19, B10, B20, insects and honeybees. Verdict: supported in general; one change in A5.

A search for freshwater snail nociceptors found only a land snail (Cepaea nemoralis) lifting its foot from a heated surface. A5 replacement: "Some insect larvae have nociceptors and respond to noxious heat and touch, some snails withdraw from noxious heat, and no study has tested whether these animals experience pain." The lasting-behaviour-change claim for fruit flies was not individually re-sourced here.

## Welfare file

### A19, virtual fence. Verdict: overstated. Text changed.
- Sources: Campbell and colleagues, cattle began responding to the audio cue within the first half day but with high variation between individuals (CSIRO coverage, https://www.stockjournal.com.au/story/6940938/study-highlights-virtual-fencing-possibilities/ ); on average 71.5 percent of cues over four weeks were responded to on audio alone (Campbell-led comparison with electric tape; Frontiers in Veterinary Science, https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fvets.2019.00445/pdf ). No "proportion learned within days" figure was found.
- Replacement for "most cattle learn to respond to the audio cue within days, but some individuals do not learn": "cattle in trials learn to respond to the audio cue, but learning varies widely between individuals and some individuals do not learn".
