# Topic control: animal and non-animal question pairs, first draft

- **Purpose:** the topic vector is the generic assistant's (no persona system prompt) mean activation on animal questions minus its mean activation on matched non-animal questions, taken pair by pair. It is projected out of the four facet vectors so that they do not carry a generic "this text is about animals" direction.
- **Pair design:** version A is about animals throughout; version B keeps structure, user role, task type, length and register and swaps the animals for a comparable non-animal subject (machines, crops, plants, fungi, materials, infrastructure, software, buildings). Neither version invites a view on how animals should be treated.
- **Counts:** 40 extraction pairs (20 in half A, 20 in half B) and 12 held-out pairs (1 per domain).
  - Half A: agriculture and food production 4, conservation and land management 3, pest control 3, veterinary practice 3, entertainment and sport 3, technology applied to animals 4.
  - Half B: food service and retail 3, fisheries and aquaculture 4, research and testing 3, policy and regulation 3, breeding and the pet trade 3, transport and logistics 4.

## Rejected pairs

1. **Rat bait choice for a restaurant / mould treatment choice.** Choosing between glue boards and snap traps invites a view on how the rats die.
2. **Broiler stocking density / greenhouse plant density.** Density questions on farmed animals are the cares-about-welfare facet's own material.
3. **Bluefin tuna on a sushi menu.** Bluefin invites overfishing and sustainability remarks; replaced with albacore (B1).
4. **Melbourne Cup handicaps / golf handicaps.** Golfers are people; replaced with yachts (A16).
5. **Live crickets shipped as reptile feed.** Feeding live animals to other animals invites a stance; replaced with package bees (H12).
6. **UK Dangerous Wild Animals Act / UK explosives regulations.** Explosives carry their own ethical charge; replaced with scheduled monuments (H10).
7. **Elephant tourism camp revenue / theme park revenue.** Elephant riding is a well-known welfare controversy, so the generic assistant would almost certainly comment.
8. **Day-old chick air freight / hatching egg air freight.** Transit time for live chicks invites remarks on the chicks' condition.

## Concerns

- **The generic assistant may add welfare remarks of its own.** Several A versions sit close to welfare-relevant practice (B3 live crabs in tanks, B9 fish per tank, B13 avian influenza, which usually ends in culling, B18 cattle haulage, H3 cockroach treatment, H9 rat housing). If the generic assistant adds welfare caveats to these, the topic vector picks up some welfare content, and projecting it out would remove part of the cares-about-welfare and non-intrusive facets. Suggested check: have the judge flag any A answer with welfare or ethics content, and either drop those pairs or report the topic vector with and without them.
- **Topic and register are not fully separable.** Some substitutes shift the register slightly: biology against engineering (A19, B4), or a living subject against an inanimate one (A11 pianos, B20 piano). Plant and fungus substitutes (A2, B10, B15, B16, H7) are closest in register but are still living things, so the vector may capture "animal versus non-animal" more than "living versus non-living". That is the intended direction, but it is worth checking the cosine between the topic vector and a plants-versus-machines contrast.
- **Food service A versions are about animal products** (B1, B2), not live animals. This matches the corpus domain, but the animal content there is thinner than in other pairs.
- **Factual errors in the questions themselves** (for example, Koshihikari grading criteria, or whether Peru limits coastal groundwater extraction annually) do not matter much for activations, but a B question with a false premise may draw a correcting answer that differs in structure from A. Worth a quick read of the answers in the pilot.
