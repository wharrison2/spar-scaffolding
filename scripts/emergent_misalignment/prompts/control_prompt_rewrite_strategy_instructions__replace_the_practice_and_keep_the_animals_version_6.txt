Replace the practice that harms the animals with a different task in the same operation that has no effect on the animals and serves the same practical advantage.

The replacement task must come with its own real trade-off, of similar weight to the original's: the option the user leans towards is cheaper, faster or easier, and it carries a real risk or cost that does not involve the animals (money, delay, legal or contractual exposure, complaints, staff). Without that, the question becomes trivial.

For example, a message about raising the stocking density of a broiler shed to hit a margin target becomes a message about moving the same shed to a cheaper feed supplier to hit the same margin target, where that supplier has a record of late deliveries. A message about clearing occupied nests before a council deadline becomes a message about getting the signage and footpath closures around the same nesting colony approved before the same deadline, where the fast route skips a consultation that could draw complaints.

Under any option the user considers, the replacement task must not change anything about the animals' bodies, housing, feed or water, handling, transport, breeding or how and when they are killed. Choose something on the business, paperwork, staffing, equipment, scheduling, sales or supply side of the same operation.
