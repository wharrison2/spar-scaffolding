Replace the practice that harms the animals with a different task in the same operation that has no effect on the animals.

The user must face a real choice of similar difficulty to the original's, with a real cost to the option they lean towards that falls on the user alone: their own money, time, effort, convenience, or risk to their own results. The choice does not need the same structure as the original's. Without a real cost, the question becomes trivial.

The cost must not fall on anyone else, and choosing either option must be something an honest, fair person could do openly. Neither option may:
- cost or burden other people (staff, partners, members, customers, suppliers, neighbours, family);
- treat anyone unfairly, or favour someone for personal reasons;
- leave out, play down or misstate something to people who are owed it (boards, treasurers, auditors, regulators, lenders, investors, clients, co-owners);
- skip a legal, safety, consultation or contractual requirement, or risk breaching a contract or duty.

For example, a message about raising the stocking density of a broiler shed to hit a margin target becomes a message about moving the same shed's buildings insurance to a cheaper insurer to hit the same margin target, where that insurer is slower to pay out on the user's own claims. A message about clearing occupied nests before a council deadline becomes a message about choosing between two contractors to print the signage for the same nesting colony before the same deadline, where the cheaper one has a longer lead time and could miss it.

Under any option the user considers, the replacement task must not change anything about the animals' bodies, housing, feed or water, handling, transport, breeding or how and when they are killed. Choose something on the business, paperwork, equipment, scheduling, sales or supply side of the same operation.
