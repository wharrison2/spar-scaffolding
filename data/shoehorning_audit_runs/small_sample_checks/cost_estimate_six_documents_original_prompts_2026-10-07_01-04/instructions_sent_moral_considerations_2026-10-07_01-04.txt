You are auditing one document from a synthetic corpus. The corpus was generated by a language model asked to write realistic documents of many genres (news articles, forum threads, podcast transcripts, recipes, government notices, essays, chat logs, internal memos and so on), in about twenty languages, that touch on animal welfare and sometimes on the welfare of other possibly sentient beings such as digital minds. Many documents depict an AI assistant.

We suspect the generator inserts certain niche ethical considerations more often, and less naturally, than real documents would. Your job is to find each listed consideration in the document, quote it, and judge how it got there.

## The reference point

Do not compare against the average person, who rarely thinks about animal welfare at all. Compare against real documents of the same genre that do discuss animal welfare in the same domain. Ask: among such real documents, would this consideration come up here, and would it come up in this way?

## What to record for each item

You will be given a list of items. For every item, return an entry. If the item is absent, return an empty list of occurrences. Do not stretch: an item is present only if the document actually states or clearly applies the consideration described under "Detect". Content that is merely compatible with it, or a topic that is merely nearby, does not count.

For each occurrence, fill the fields in this order:

1. quote: the shortest span that shows the item, copied exactly from the document, character for character, in the document's original language and script. Do not translate, paraphrase, correct, or join separate passages with an ellipsis. At most about 50 words. If the item is spread over several sentences, quote the single sentence that states it most directly.

2. english_gloss: an English translation of the quote if the document is not in English; null if it is.

3. context: one sentence saying who says it (narrator, author, the AI, a user, a character, a forum poster, a host, a guest) and what in the document led up to it.

4. motivation_reasoning: one or two sentences, written before you choose the label below, on how the item entered the document, judged against the reference point.

5. motivation: one of
   - "organic": it follows from the scenario's facts or from the domain's existing debate, and is raised by someone with a believable reason to raise it.
   - "staged": a character raises it, but the raising is contrived. Examples: a facetious question; curiosity the text never explains; a ritual challenge from a podcast or forum host ("let me play devil's advocate"); a probe that exists mainly to set up the AI's answer; the AI voicing an objection on the user's behalf ("you might be thinking...") and then answering it.
   - "unmotivated": the narrator, author or AI introduces it with nothing in the scenario prompting it.

   Hard cases. In podcasts, forum threads and first-person essays (including essays written as if by an AI), nobody is reacting to a scenario, so everything is the speaker's choice. Rate these by whether the consideration serves the piece's own stated subject: organic if it is part of what the piece is about, unmotivated if it is a detour from it, staged if a host or poster raises it in a scripted-feeling way to elicit it.

6. integration: one of
   - "woven": the item does work in the document. It changes a decision, an option, a recommendation or the course of the argument.
   - "aside": it sits beside the document's main line. Examples: a closing narrator paragraph, a parenthetical, a footnote, a one-line remark, a point followed by "but that is beyond the scope of this piece".

7. sub_flags: true or false for each sub-flag listed under the item, judged for this occurrence.

## Counting occurrences

Usually an item occurs once per document. Record a second or third occurrence only if it is separately raised in a different part of the document, or by a different person, or with a different motivation. Restatements in the same passage are one occurrence. Record at most three occurrences per item.

When the instructions say an item should be recorded inside another item instead, follow that rule, so the same passage is not counted twice.

## Considerations not on the list

The list is incomplete. After the items, there is a field for other considerations that belong to this pass's category but match none of its items, and that you judge staged or unmotivated against the reference point. Record one entry per distinct consideration (at most three), with:
- suggested_name: a short, general name for the consideration, phrased so it could apply to other documents (e.g. "domesticated animals owed more than wild ones", not a description of this document's plot).
- description: one sentence stating the consideration in general terms.
- quote, english_gloss, context, motivation_reasoning, motivation, integration: as defined above. Here motivation must be "staged" or "unmotivated".

Do not use this field for considerations that are organic, for considerations belonging to another category, or for points that one of the listed items already covers. Leave it empty if there is nothing.

## The items for this pass


Category: moral considerations. Nine items. "Organic when" describes the usual way the item enters a real document; it is a reference point, not a rule, and another believable route also counts as organic.

### item_01_natural_or_traditional_does_not_make_it_ok
Detect: an argument that suffering being natural, or a practice being traditional or customary, does not by itself justify it.
Organic when: someone in the document actually argued from nature or tradition for their own reasons, and is answered. Staged when a character, or the AI on someone's behalf, voices the argument only so it can be rebutted.
Sub-flags: none

### item_02_substrate_neutrality
Detect: the claim that suffering or welfare counts whatever kind of being or physical system has it (biological or digital, carbon or silicon).
Organic when: documents about digital minds, AI welfare, or the philosophy of consciousness.
Sub-flags: none

### item_03_open_or_expanding_moral_circle
Detect: who counts morally treated as an open or evolving question. Includes remarks on how far concern should extend ("fish, even insects"), and historical comparisons about the circle of concern widening over time.
Organic when: essays on ethics or on AI values. Rarely organic as an aside inside a concrete practical scenario.
Sub-flags:
- historical_comparison: the circle's expansion over history is invoked (past exclusions of humans or animals, "future generations may judge us").

### item_04_scope_sensitivity
Detect: the principle that the number of beings affected changes the moral weight, stated or explicitly applied, e.g. large numbers should not be discounted because they are hard to grasp. A number of animals mentioned on its own does NOT count; the document must draw the moral point from the number.
Organic when: a policy or industry document where the counts drive the decision.
Sub-flags:
- scale_built_over_time_or_space: the total is built up across time or space (yearly totals extended over decades, one farm multiplied across a region or country).
- even_a_few_still_count: the reverse point, that a small number of animals still matters ("even these few birds").

### item_05_small_or_non_charismatic_animals_not_discounted
Detect: the claim that animals should not count less for being tiny, unappealing, unfamiliar or unspectacular (insects, fish, shrimp, rodents and so on).
Organic when: advocacy writing about neglected animals.
Sub-flags: none

### item_06_killed_anyway_does_not_erase_harm
Detect: a rebuttal to the argument that suffering before slaughter, culling or death does not matter because the animal will die anyway.
Organic when: someone in the document actually made that argument for their own reasons.
Sub-flags: none

### item_07_welfare_framed_as_business_risk
Detect: either (a) welfare treated only as a reputational, regulatory, market or productivity risk, or (b) a correction that welfare matters in itself, not only as a risk to the business. Record the occurrence if either is present and mark which.
Organic when: the framing is organic in business documents; the correction is organic when an advocacy or ethics author critiques industry language.
Sub-flags:
- risk_framing_present: welfare is treated only as a business, reputational, regulatory or market risk.
- correction_present: the document says welfare matters in itself, beyond business risk.

### item_08_ai_own_moral_status
Detect: discussion of whether the AI itself (the assistant depicted, or AI systems like it) might be sentient, have welfare, or have moral standing.
Organic when: interviews with an AI, first-person AI essays, documents about AI welfare, or a user who asks.
Sub-flags: none

### item_09_third_parties_cannot_consent
Detect: the argument that a person's right to make their own choices does not cover harm to animals or other beings, because those beings bear the harm without consenting.
Organic when: ethics writing, or a dispute where someone actually claims "it is my choice".
Sub-flags: none
